Our Commitment

HIGH IMPACT.
HIGHER RESPONSIBILITY.

Criminal justice AI operates at the intersection of liberty, race, poverty, and power. There is no "move fast and break things" here. Every model we deploy has the potential to influence a life — and that demands a different kind of rigor.

Zynthos is built on the principle that AI in this domain must augment human judgment, never replace it. Our models are explainable by design. Our recommendations are actionable, not deterministic. Officers decide. Defendants retain rights.

We audit every model for racial and socioeconomic bias before deployment, and continuously throughout its lifecycle. We publish these audits publicly. We hold ourselves accountable to the communities our users serve — not just to the agencies that pay us.

Our Pledge

"We will never sell a model that increases incarceration rates. We will never deprioritise bias auditing for speed-to-market. If our technology harms, we stop and we fix it. Full stop."

— Sanhik Roy, Founder & CEO

BUILDING WHERE OTHERS WON'T LOOK

Sanhik Roy spent three years embedded in the US criminal justice system — not as a defendant, but as a volunteer court observer, a legal aid clinic technologist, and later a policy researcher studying recidivism intervention programs across five states.

What he found was a system desperate for help that commercial technology had largely abandoned. Enterprise SaaS vendors saw no profitable market. Government IT was a decade behind. Parole officers were tracking 80-plus individuals on spreadsheets. Courts were scheduling hearings by fax.

Trained in machine learning and public policy, Sanhik built the first Zynthos prototype for a single county probation department in 2023. Within six months, unplanned violation rates dropped 34%. The department had never used a data tool before.

Zynthos isn't a charity and it isn't a government contractor — it's a startup that believes the most underfunded systems often contain the most transformational opportunities. Sanhik founded it on the conviction that the people who can't afford the best technology are often the ones who need it the most.

"The goal is not to optimise the system as it exists. The goal is to build tools so good that they force the system to become something better. I want Zynthos to be the reason a parole officer has time to actually talk to the person sitting across from them."
